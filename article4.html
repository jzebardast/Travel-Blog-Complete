Mount Fuji: Japan's Sacred Peak

The most iconic mountain and an active volcano located about 100 kilometers southwest of Tokyo. Written with the characters for "wealth" and "samurai," Fuji's name evokes a sense of strength, honor, and enduring prestige.

I've been traveling since 2017, chasing moments instead of plans. I'm fascinated by snakes, love sharks, would happily spend weeks deep in the jungle, and can never get enough papaya. As long as there's a next destination, I know I'm exactly where I'm meant to be.

Mount Fuji rises 3,776 meters above sea level, its near-perfect cone visible from Tokyo on a clear day. For centuries, it has been a place of pilgrimage, a subject for painters like Hokusai, and a symbol woven into the identity of an entire country.

The best views often come not from the summit itself, but from a respectful distance — a five-story pagoda framing the peak, or fields of autumn leaves leading the eye toward the snow-capped cone above the clouds.

💡 Pro tipps
  1. Visit at sunrise for the clearest skies — cloud cover tends to build up by early afternoon.
  2. The official climbing season runs July to early September; outside these months, trails are closed for safety.

Whether you climb it, photograph it from a nearby town, or simply admire it from the train window on your way to Kyoto, Mount Fuji has a way of making you feel small in the best possible sense.
